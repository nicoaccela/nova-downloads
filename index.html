What Nova does

One assistant that keeps the whole day moving.

Nova lives in a terminal session on your Mac. You talk to it, it answers out loud, and it hands the heavy lifting to workers it keeps an eye on.

Runs a fleet of workers

Splits big jobs across parallel Claude Code workers, hears the moment each one finishes, and keeps them moving. You hear about whole results, not every step.

Talks out loud

A natural voice (Kokoro) and local speech recognition, both running on your Mac. Talk hands free, or have any reply read to you.

Texts you on Telegram

From your own bot, which answers only you. Reach Nova from your phone between meetings and get short, phone-sized replies.

Remembers what matters

Decisions, people, open loops and where you left off, kept as plain files on your Mac. It picks up the thread after a restart.

Plugs into your work tools

Microsoft 365, Salesforce, Pursuit and Slack, each with your own sign-in. Nova reads first and asks before it changes anything.

Tuned to your role

Setup reads the notes and skills you already keep for Claude Code and shapes Nova around your work, your cadence and how you like things said.

What gets set up

Everything Nova needs, and nothing it doesn't.

Grouped the way the installer builds it. The optional parts are asked about during setup, so you only get what you say yes to.

Core

  • The nova launcher and a pinned terminal session to come back to
  • A persona and preferences written from your answers
  • A start-of-session hook that restores context
  • A model and effort setting you can change later

Fleet

  • The fleet command for parallel workers in tmux
  • Silent finish pings, so Nova knows when a worker is done
  • A watcher that brings each ping to Nova
  • An orchestration playbook and a brief template

Memory

  • A local memory folder with recall and remember
  • Handoff notes, so a fresh session picks up where you left off
  • A catch-up routine when you come back

Voice Optional

  • Kokoro text to speech and Whisper speech recognition
  • Both listen on this Mac only
  • Read-aloud you can switch with nova-voice on or off
  • Pick your own voice

Telegram Optional

  • Your own bot, created by you in Telegram
  • Paired to your account only, allowlist of one
  • A nova-telegram on or off switch
  • Phone approvals stay off unless you turn them on

Work tools Optional

  • Microsoft 365, Salesforce (read only), Pursuit and Slack
  • You sign in to each one yourself
  • One read-only test call each, to prove it works

Nova's own skills

  • Fleet orchestration and the dispatch and ping rhythm
  • Worker org, handoff and memory keeping
  • Voice mode, the Telegram channel and the persona
  • The daily rhythm pieces Nova runs

Starts with your Mac Asks first

  • Nova opens in your terminal when you log in
  • The fleet watcher runs in the background

Doctor

  • A check of every part, pass or fail, line by line

How it goes

Five steps. One of them is typing.

~40minutes

Most of it is the voice download and your sign-ins. Claude Code walks you through each step and stops to check before moving on.

  • Access and the command~5 min
  • Claude Code builds your Nova~10 min
  • Guided steps and hello~25 min
  1. 1

    Get access to the repo a few minutes

    Nova's installer lives in a private GitHub repo. Ask the person who shared this page to add your GitHub account.

  2. 2

    Paste one command 10 seconds

    It clones the repo and opens Claude Code there with one request: set up Nova. The command is below.

  3. 3

    Claude Code finds your context and builds your Nova about 10 minutes

    It reads the notes and skills you already keep (read only, it leaves them as they are), asks a few questions about your role, shows you the plan, then installs each part and backs up anything it touches.

  4. 4

    A few guided steps about 20 minutes

    • Your Telegram bot. Create it with BotFather, enter the token in a local prompt, then pair it to your account.
    • Your sign-ins. Sign in to each work tool with your own account. If one needs an app id, your IT provides it.
    • Mic permission. Allow your terminal app to use the microphone, so you can talk to Nova.
  5. 5

    Nova says hello, out loud a few seconds

    A last doctor check, then Nova introduces itself in its new voice and asks what you want to start on.

The one command

Paste this in your terminal.

Claude Code takes it from there and stops to ask before anything that matters.

$gh repo clone nicoaccela/nova ~/Projects/nova && cd ~/Projects/nova && claude "set up Nova"

This needs access to the private repo. If the clone says "not found", ask the person who shared this page to add your GitHub account, then run it again.

Before you start

What you need, what stays yours, and how to undo it.

Requirements

  • A Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 or newer)
  • Homebrew Setup adds tmux, jq, uv, Bun and ffmpeg if they are missing.
  • Claude Code, signed in with your own account
  • GitHub CLI (gh), signed in, with access to the repo
  • About 3 GB free for the voice models
  • Telegram on your phone Only if you want to text Nova.
  • Mac awake and online Nova lives on your Mac. To reach it from your phone, keep the lid open and on power.

Privacy

  • Everything is local. Memory is plain files in your home folder. Voice runs on your Mac and listens on this Mac only.
  • Your logins stay yours. Every tool signs in with your own account. The repo holds no logins, tokens or app ids.
  • Your bot answers only you. Its token stays on your Mac, and pairing limits it to your account.
  • Nothing goes out without your yes. Nova drafts, reads and prepares. Sending, posting and deleting wait for you.

What you can undo

All of it. Every file setup changes is backed up first, and its additions sit in clearly marked blocks.

To remove Nova, open Claude Code in the repo and say uninstall Nova, or run ./setup.sh --uninstall. It restores your backups and lists anything left behind, like Homebrew packages or the voice models, so you can decide.

Or switch off just one part: nova-voice off, nova-telegram off, or sign out of a tool.
